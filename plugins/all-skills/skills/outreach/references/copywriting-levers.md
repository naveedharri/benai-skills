## 1. The 4-sentence email framework

Every first email is built from four moves. Keep it to roughly that, under ~100 words total (75 is the sweet spot).

1. **Why you / why now.** The relevance hook. Why this person, why this moment. This is where the personalized opener lives (see `personalization-categories.md`).
2. **How you help.** The concrete value, an outcome, not a feature list.
3. **Social proof.** A named client plus a metric, brief.
4. **CTA.** One clear ask that requires a reply, not a click.

Follow-ups are shorter and add one new angle each; they do not restate the whole pitch.

## 2. The 17 copywriting levers

Pick the one or two that fit the audience and the proof you have. Do not stack all of them; a lever is a primary angle, not a checklist.

| # | Lever | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What's in it for them (WIIFT) | Always underneath; every sentence passes "so what?" |
| 2 | Niche down on outcome/value | Specific claims beat broad ones; credibility |
| 3 | Lead magnet / front-end offer | Low-friction ask (free audit, tool, resource) |
| 4 | Outcome / case study | You have a strong, quantified result |
| 5 | Pain messaging ("poke the bear") | Pain-aware audience; name pain, agitate, imply fix |
| 6 | Direct offer template | "We helped {type} get {result} in {time} without {risk}" |
| 7 | Trigger messaging | A real event: new role, hiring, funding, tech installed |
| 8 | "So what?" second-order effects | Follow the problem to its painful consequence |
| 9 | Problem sniffing | You found a specific concrete problem (broken page, stale content) |
| 10 | Direct punchy | C-suite; 1 to 2 sentences, no preamble |
| 11 | Information arbitrage | You have data they don't (benchmark, competitor intel) |
| 12 | Speak to implicit objections | Address the unsaid concern ("I know you've tried X") |
| 13 | Relevantly funny | Industry-specific humor, never random |
| 14 | Tastefully provocative | Challenge an assumption the segment holds |
| 15 | Founder story / market opinion | The sender is a founder with a real POV |
| 16 | Lean into the mechanism | The "how" is genuinely differentiated and interesting |
| 17 | Attention hacking | Pattern interrupt: a question, an unexpected format |

Selection shortcuts: strong proof -> 4 or 6; pain-aware -> 5 + 8; unique data -> 11; founder sending -> 15; C-suite -> 10; contrarian angle -> 14.

## 3. Hard rules (non-negotiable)

- **Length:** email body under ~100 words; 75 is ideal. Follow-ups 2 to 4 sentences.
- **Subject lines:** 2 to 3 words, lowercase-ish, curiosity not clickbait ("quick question", "idea for {company}"). Offer 2 options per email as spintax.
- **No "Hope this finds you well"** or any throat-clearing opener; it is a spam signal and wastes the first line.
- **Do not open with "Hi, I'm [name]..."** Lead with value, not a resume.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons, or restructure.
- **No corporate jargon:** synergize, leverage, best-in-class, cutting-edge, circle back, touch base.
- **No leading questions** ("Wouldn't you love to...").
- **CTA must require a reply, not a click.** No links in cold email bodies; links tank deliverability and read as a pitch.
- **Spintax is mandatory** in every email: `{Hi|Hey|Hello}`, varied connectors, varied CTAs. It stops the sending platform from fingerprinting identical sends, which protects the inbox placement the whole campaign depends on.
- **Merge tags only for real data fields:** `{{firstName}}`, `{{company}}`, `{{jobTitle}}`, `{{industry}}`. Never a merge tag you cannot reliably fill.
- **Sign-off is the sender's name** (e.g. "Aryan"), never `{{firstName}}` (that is the recipient).

## 4. Variant strategy (A/B/C)

Write three versions of the first email, each led by a different primary lever, so the campaign can learn what the segment responds to.

- 1A, 1B, 1C: same offer and CTA shape, different angle (e.g. A = case study, B = poke-the-bear, C = information arbitrage).
- Each variant gets 2 to 3 personalization suggestions noting where the personalized line goes (see `personalization-categories.md`).
- Keep the proof and CTA consistent across variants so the test isolates the angle.

## 5. Sequence structure and timing

Maximum 4 to 6 steps per channel. A clean default email sequence:

| Email | Thread | Timing | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New thread | Day 0 | Best shot, most replies |
| 2 | Reply to 1 | +3 business days | Short bump, one new proof point or angle (2 to 4 sentences) |
| 3 | New thread, new subject | +5 business days | Different angle from 1 (if 1 = pain, 3 = proof) |
| 4 | Reply to 3 | +3 business days | Final, gentle, low pressure. Not a "breakup" guilt-trip |

For a 6-step sequence, extend the same logic: alternate new-thread and reply, rotate angle each step, keep raising a new proof point rather than repeating. Multichannel cadences interleave LinkedIn and calls between emails, see `cadence-and-channels.md`.

---

## 6. A/B test plan

- Split traffic 33/33/33 across 1A/1B/1C.
- Minimum ~150 contacts per variant for a readable signal.
- Winner = highest **positive** reply rate (ignore "unsubscribe" and "not interested").
- Run 4 to 6 weeks, then kill the losers and run the winner at full volume.

## 7. Offer and front-end offer selection

Six core B2B value angles: save time, save money, make more money, reduce risk, raise status, (rarely) live longer/safer. Map the offer to one or two.

Front-end offer (what the CTA asks for) by situation:

| Situation | Front-end offer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No proof yet | Lead magnet / free resource | Lowest friction |
| Strong case studies | Direct offer with the result | Proof carries it |
| High price, long cycle | Free audit / assessment | Foot in the door |
| Competitive market | Free trial / pilot | Let the product differentiate |
| Warm / aware | Direct meeting request | Trust already exists |
| Price-sensitive SMB | Free tool / calculator | Value first |

---

## 8. Reply-rate benchmarks

So expectations are honest:

- **Best in class:** 1 positive reply per 10 to 50 contacts (tight targeting, strong proof, great copy).
- **Average:** 1 per 250 to 350.
- **Struggling:** 1 per 500 to 1000+.
- **Deliverability floor:** if total reply rate (excluding out-of-office) is under ~1%, fix deliverability before touching copy; the messages are not being seen.

## 9. Output format for emails.md

Use this structure so the file is ready to paste or import:

```markdown
# Campaign: <name> / <ICP segment>

Strategy: <1 to 2 lines on the approach and primary levers>

## Email 1A - <label> (<primary lever>)
Subject: {option one|option two}

<body with spintax and merge tags>

Word count: <n> | Levers: <list>
Personalization slot: <which sentence, which personalization type>

## Email 1B - <label> (<lever>)
...

## Email 1C - <label> (<lever>)
...

## A/B test plan
Split 33/33/33 | 150+ per variant | winner = positive reply rate | 4 to 6 weeks

## Email 2 (reply, +3 days)
...
## Email 3 (new thread, +5 days)
...
## Email 4 (reply, +3 days)
...
```
