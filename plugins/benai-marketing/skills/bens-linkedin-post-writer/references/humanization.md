# Humanization Pass

Run the Step 5 draft through this pass before showing it to the user. Do NOT skip it. Do NOT
claim you "applied the principles internally". Actually walk through every check below and edit
the draft.

If a separate `/humanizer` skill is available on the harness, also run it. But these rules must
be applied either way. They are the source of truth for this skill and remove the most common
AI tells in Ben's content.

## Words and phrases to remove entirely

Never use these in the final post. They are AI tells that immediately read as machine-written:

- "unlock", "shift" (as in mindset shift / paradigm shift), "elevate", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "navigate the landscape", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in", "game-changer", "landscape" (when used as a metaphor)
- "chatbot", "flag" / "flags" (as in red flags), "scream" (as in "this screams X")
- "Here's the thing you need to understand"
- "I'm excited to share..." or any LinkedIn cliche opener
- "It's not just X, it's Y" (and related negative parallelism variants, see below)

## Banned structural patterns

- **No em dashes anywhere.** Use hyphens, commas, semicolons, or line breaks instead. This applies inside checklist items, body paragraphs, and CTAs. Em dashes are one of the most reliable AI tells.
- **No rule-of-three fragments.** Do not stack three short parallel fragments for rhythm. "Fast. Cheap. Reliable." or "It builds. It tests. It ships." reads as AI. Pick one and let it land, or rewrite as a normal sentence.
- **No negative parallelisms.** Banned constructions include:
  - "It's not X, it's Y"
  - "Not only X but Y"
  - "Guide, don't decide" / "Lead, don't follow" style imperative pairs
- **No bullet lists for problems or misconceptions.** Problems must be written as flowing prose. Arrow bullets are only for actionable steps, features, or how-it-works breakdowns.
- **No vague attributions.** Don't write "experts say", "many believe", "studies show" without a source. If Ben wouldn't name the source, don't make the claim.
- **No inflated symbolism or grand metaphors.** "This is more than a tool, it's a revolution" type lines must be cut.
- **No promotional adjective stacking.** "Powerful, intuitive, beautifully designed" → cut to one specific claim.
- **No "-ing analysis" filler.** "Building scalable systems. Empowering teams. Driving growth." → cut.
- **No excessive conjunctive phrases.** "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," at the start of sentences signals AI structuring. Remove them and let the next sentence stand on its own.

## CTA-specific rules

- **CTAs that reference a video must describe what the video actually contains.** Do not write "Watch the full walkthrough" or "Check out the full video" without saying what's in it. Write what the viewer will actually see: "I show the exact n8n nodes, the Airtable schema, and the prompt I'm using" not "Watch the full walkthrough."
- **Never mention AI as the author.** Do not write "I had AI help me draft this" or "with AI's help" or anything that reveals AI is involved in writing the post. The post is Ben's voice; AI involvement is invisible.

## Final read-aloud check

After all the edits above, read the post out loud (or simulate doing so). Ask:

1. Does this sound like a person texting a smart friend, or like a press release?
2. Is there any sentence where the rhythm feels too symmetrical or too tidy?
3. Is there any line that an AI would write that a person wouldn't?
4. Does every line earn its place?

If any answer is "no" on 1, or "yes" on 2/3, or "no" on 4, revise before presenting.

## After-writing iteration questions

Present the humanized post in an artifact. Then ask the user:

- How does this feel? Want to adjust the tone, length, or emphasis?
- Should we sharpen any section?
- Want to try a different hook from the ones we explored?

The first draft is a starting point, not the final product.
