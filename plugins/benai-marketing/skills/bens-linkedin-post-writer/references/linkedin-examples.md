## Example 3: The Prioritization Framework

**Type:** Personal / Life Advice

2 years ago, I changed the way I set goals.

And it changed my life.

Instead of a list of goals, I defined what parts in life I was going to prioritize.

But more importantly, I defined what I was **NOT** going to prioritize.

Why?

Because most ambitious people fail on their goals, not because they are not motivated.

But because they simply set too many.

You can't get a business off the ground, have your best social life, travel the world, **and** be in the best shape of your life.

So every year, I look at this list below and I rate myself from 1-10 on each of the points for the past year.

Then I pick 2 out of these 9 points in my life that I want to prioritize more over the next year.

Health:
➝ Mind
➝ Soul
➝ Body

Social:
➝ Romance
➝ Family
➝ Friends

Work:
➝ Mission
➝ Money
➝ Growth

Why does it work?

In the era of social media, FOMO is real. You will always be reminded of what you don't have. What you are **NOT** doing.

And prioritizing one area will mean another might suffer a bit. That isn't failure, that is a trade-off.

So when FOMO thoughts creep in, reminding yourself of the priorities you set instantly resolves your doubts.

2 years ago, I prioritized my mission and body. I wanted to quit my job, get my business off the ground, and get in good shape.

So when doubts started creeping in on a Sunday behind my desk while my friends were out, I knew I was doing the right thing.

And I succeeded in both.

This year I prioritized money and family.

Did I travel the world, spend hours building friendships, or find perfect mental balance? No.

But I earned more than I ever have and spent more time with family than in the last decade.

The same principle applies to building a business. You have to pick your season.

If you are just starting out, your season is lead generation.

You have to be fine not knowing every AI tool, being underpaid every once in a while, or your product not being perfect.

Those are the trades you make to win.

> "Most people overestimate what they can do in a year, but underestimate what they can do in 5."

Instead of trying to win every category at once, this system helps me build a life where different areas get their turn, one year at a time.

Pick your priorities. Be okay with the trade-offs.

What are yours for 2026? 🎯

## Example 8: Agency to SaaS Path

**Type:** AI Agency Advice / Actionable

One of the best paths to a successful AI SaaS?
Starting as an AI Automation Agency. Here's why 👇

As an automation agency, you take on custom projects. You solve real problems for different companies.

And quickly, you'll notice patterns.

Some solutions you create are reusable and deliver high ROI for other companies.

It's a great path to a SaaS.

Here's the path:

1️⃣ Build Custom: Start by creating tailored solutions for different companies.

2️⃣ Identify Reusability: Notice common pain points and high-ROI solutions that other businesses could benefit from.

3️⃣ Resell: Take those solutions, customize them again, and implement them for new clients.

4️⃣ Refine & Optimize: With each implementation, improve the solution based on real-world feedback and learnings.

5️⃣ Productize: Gradually standardize the solution while maintaining its value.

6️⃣ Build SaaS: Once you've nailed the solution, transition it into a SaaS product.

Here's why this approach works:

✅ Feedback & Iterations: Good SaaS products are built from continuous feedback and iterations.

✅ Deep Customer Insights: Working closely with clients helps you dive deep into common pain points.

✅ Proven Market Fit: By the time you create a SaaS, you already know you solve a pain point.

✅ Low Risk: Generate revenue through services while slowly building your product.

✅ Testimonials: Happy clients from your agency work become your first SaaS advocates.

If you're starting an AI agency, keep your eyes open for those repeatable solutions.

They might just be your ticket to a successful SaaS.

## Example 10: Newsletter Automation Playbook

**Type:** Tutorial / How-To

95% of newsletter automations produce "generic" content.

Because they skip the hardest part → context engineering.

We built and productized a full newsletter service with n8n + Airtable.

Here's the exact 5-step playbook you can copy:

1. Onboard for context ↓
Collect company overview, ICP, tone rules, 2–3 reference newsletters, and personal backstory.
That's what makes outputs unique to each brand.

2. Generate ideas ↓
Do it three ways → user insights, repurposed links, or AI idea finder.
Keeps the content pipeline full and relevant.

3. Assign context to each step ↓
Idea → subject line → draft → images.
Feed the right info at the right stage. Don't dump everything into one prompt.

4. Automate workflows ↓
Use n8n for flows, Airtable to store context + outputs, and insert QA checkpoints.
Automation handles the repetition → humans ensure quality.

5. Package the service ↓
Turn it into a product → setup, monthly deliverables, add-ons.
Sell on outcomes (published newsletters), not tasks.

The truth: AI doesn't remove the work.
It reshapes it.

Quality comes from context, testing, and human-in-the-loop.

## Key Patterns Across LinkedIn Posts

### Opening Patterns

↳ Bold statement or counterintuitive insight
↳ Personal confession or vulnerability
↳ Direct challenge to common beliefs
↳ "I built [system] with [tools]" (system showcase opener)
↳ "[X]% of [group] still [mistake]" (stat-based challenge)

### Formatting

↳ Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences max)
↳ Line breaks between every thought
↳ Arrow bullets (`↳` or `➝`) for lists
↳ Bold Unicode text for headers (`𝗛𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿`)

### Recurring Phrases

↳ "Here's the thing:"
↳ "99% will... Be the 1%."
↳ "I learned this the hard way."
↳ "Domain expertise + AI = Unfair advantage"
↳ "in under 2 hours 👇"
↳ "Comment '[keyword]' and I'll send it"
↳ "Here's the exact [X]-step playbook"

### Engagement Hooks

↳ Comment "[keyword]" to get [resource]
↳ Questions at the end ("What are yours?")
↳ Soft CTA to Accelerator
↳ "Comment '[keyword]' and I'll send it to you / your way"

### Content Types

↳ Insight / Mindset Shift (most common)
↳ Tutorial / How-To (step-by-step)
↳ Personal / Life Advice
↳ AI Agency Advice
↳ System Showcase / Build Demo (highest engagement — "I built X" format)
