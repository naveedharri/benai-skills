## Audience context

Ben's audience is primarily ambitious solopreneurs, career pivoters, exploring entrepreneurs,
and AI agency builders. They care about:

- Building real, working AI systems they can replicate
- Practical playbooks they can extract and use
- Time-to-result (short timeframes that prove "I can do this too")
- Tool stacks (Claude, n8n, Airtable, Make, Supabase, etc.)
- Going from learning to earning

They do NOT care about pure inspiration, lifestyle content, or generic productivity advice.
They are here for AI systems.

## Highest-performing hooks

| Rank | Hook Type | Pattern | Why it works |
|------|-----------|---------|--------------|
| 1 | **Achievement / Proof** | "I built [thing] with [tools] in [time] 👇" | Specific, tangible, instantly credible. Tool names + time constraint = curiosity + proof. Best for likes and shares. |
| 2 | **Contrarian Stat** | "[X]% of [audience] still [common mistake]" | Creates "am I in the X%?" self-assessment. People comment to prove they're in the minority. Best for comments. |

## Highest-performing CTAs

| Rank | CTA Type | Pattern | Best for |
|------|----------|---------|----------|
| 1 | **Comment Trigger** | "Comment '[keyword]' and I'll send it" | Comments + total engagement (algorithm fuel) |
| 2 | **Direct Link** | Links to templates, tutorials, community | Likes (passive engagement, save + click without commenting) |

## The specificity rule

The #1 differentiator between top and bottom hooks is **specificity**:

- "I built an AI SaaS with Claude + n8n in under 2 hours" beats "I'm done building skills in one shot"
- "97% of agencies still treat AI like a toy" beats "Most businesses still waste days on SEO content"

Vague subjects ("Most businesses", "Agencies don't") without numbers or specifics consistently
underperform. Replace generic placeholders with precise numbers, names, timeframes, and outcomes.

## The utility rule

Every top-performing framework delivers a tangible, extractable asset: template, playbook,
checklist, or system. Bottom-performing frameworks deliver opinions, stories, or observations
without a concrete takeaway. The audience rewards utility, not inspiration. If the post doesn't
promise something the reader can extract and use, it will underperform.

---

## Patterns that consistently underperform (avoid)

- **Personal Revelation hooks**: "I'm done [doing X]" / "I changed [Y]" get consistently low engagement
- **Generic Observation hooks**: "Most [audience] [do X]" without a specific number
- **Personal Insight / Life Advice frameworks**: personal story, framework, motivational close
- **Problem-Agitate without a concrete Solve**: diagnosis without a blueprint
- **Comparison (Old vs New) without a tangible deliverable**
- **Off-topic content** (lifestyle, travel, life advice): Ben's audience follows for AI systems
- **Passive "link in comments"** without naming what it links to
- **Open-ended question CTA only** ("What are yours?") with no value exchange
- **No CTA at all**
