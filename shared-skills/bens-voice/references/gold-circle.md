## Deep advice (100+ words)

### 14. Rate-my-build with one principled improvement (#25)
Context: member shares his finished product website asking for feedback.

> Hi Tobias, looks really good. I like the design. I like the general layout and copy. What I would make clearer and what I generally recommend to do with “new” products (products people are not that familiar with yet), which I would consider this product is to have the “what it is” clearly described earlier in the page.
>
> With new and innovative products people are not yet familiar with, this is often important to do besides describing the benefits which you already have, because people need to be educated before understanding the benefits.
>
> So having a one-line H2 or H1 title that actually clearly describes what it does or what it is high up on the page is I think important so all of the rest makes a lot more sense for the user.

### 16. Tool-landscape breakdown (#47)
Context: member asks when to use Cowork vs Make vs Relevance vs Hermes.

> Hi Purti.
>
> Claude Cowork, Codex, Hermes are all agent harnesses, they are best to use for one-off tasks and automating your own internal work. You honestly can choose for yourself which one you like. Most will have the same functionalities. For clients, it only really makes sense setting up or building inside of these tools if they actually already work or have adopted one of these tools internally.
>
> Make.com and N8N are deterministic automation platforms. They can be great to build automation solutions that can run autonomously, have high volume, and have a set and specific workflow. The advantage is that this can be far cheaper to run, especially for these high-volume automations. For example automatic lead enrichment etc. These are also great to build for clients because you can deploy them even though a company doesn't use any of these agent harnesses yet. It can be cheaper to run.
>
> It's a good idea. I might add a video to the course where I give my breakdown on which tools to use for what.

### 17. Intro with deep positioning advice (#84)
Context: Salesforce consultant intro, asking how to start in AI.

> Hi @Keahi Selhorst!
>
> Great to have you, and amazing background!
>
> Two things I generally recommend when trying to start in AI are:
>
> Stick to your strengths. You already are obviously very good with building systems, and I can imagine you have a lot of experience optimizing sales operations.
>
> Go service-led and be flexible. It's easy to fall into the trap of building products or scalable solutions, but the reality of selling AI right now is that it requires a lot of training and consulting in order for companies to actually get value out of these systems. Whether you lean more towards the training side or the building side depends a bit on the customer and where they are at, but almost every client will require a combination.
>
> So generally, you want to be flexible, look at what each specific customer needs, focus on delivering quick wins to get your foot in the door, and often this leads into long-term AI partnerships.
>
> I think you'll enjoy the weekly Q&A's where we often discuss these types of topics.
