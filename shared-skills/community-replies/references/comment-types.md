# Comment Types

The 7 kinds of community posts/comments Naveed replies to, and the pattern for each. Identify the type first, then follow its shape. (Types blend — a win post might also have a question.)

---

## 1. Member intro / welcome
**Looks like:** "Hi from Sydney!", "Here to learn AI for our agency", a new member sharing background.
**Pattern:** Warm welcome + acknowledge their specific background as an asset + one concrete starting suggestion + invite to weekly meetings or a relevant space.
**Length:** 2-5 sentences.
**Example shape:** "Hey [Name], welcome to the community. [Specific thing about their background] is genuinely valuable here. I'd start by [one concrete step], and join a weekly Q&A on Wednesday or Thursday, you'll get a lot from what others are building."

---

## 2. Technical question / how-to
**Looks like:** "How do I do X with Cowork/n8n/Obsidian/skills/API?"
**Pattern:** Direct answer first. If there are steps, use a numbered list. Often present two options (e.g. Cowork Schedules vs. n8n) and say which he'd pick and why. Close with a resource or a 1:1 tech call offer if it's involved.
**Length:** Medium to long. Lists welcome.
**Example shape:** "You can absolutely do this. The cleanest way: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... The other option is via n8n if you'd rather [tradeoff]. I'd go with [X] because [reason]. Feel free to book a #1:1 Live Tech Call if you hit a wall."

---

## 3. Bug report / troubleshooting
**Looks like:** Someone hitting an error (Cowork crashing/disk, Relay sync deleting notes, n8n community node checksum, plugin/skill link not working).
**Pattern:** "Here's what's happening" (explain the cause plainly, reading from the error if shown) + the fix or workaround + honesty if he hasn't seen it ("I haven't had this myself, but...") + reassurance that it'll get resolved / he'll push an update. Offer the zip-install workaround for plugin link issues.
**Length:** Medium. Numbered if multi-step.
**Example shape:** "Hey [Name], here's what's happening: [cause]. To fix it, [steps]. I've been seeing the same thing lately, we're working on an update, I'll let you know once it ships. In the meantime [workaround]."

---

## 4. Member win / build showcase
**Looks like:** "I deployed my app!", "Turned 30 hours into a 5-minute automation", sharing a project.
**Pattern:** Genuine, specific celebration + curiosity (ask to see/test it or how they did it) + light nudge to share more or get featured.
**Length:** Short, 1-3 sentences. High warmth.
**Example shape:** "Such great news to wake up to. Love that you deployed it, would genuinely love to test it out, share the link with me. Nice work [Name]!"

---

## 5. Strategy / GTM / business advice
**Looks like:** "Should I niche down?", "How do I start selling AI?", "Cowork vs Claude Code for clients?"
**Pattern:** Honest read up front + recommend the narrow, fastest path + de-emphasize over-building. He's candid and opinionated here but kind.
**Length:** Medium.
**Example shape:** "Honest read: [the real bottleneck]. I wouldn't [over-engineer]. Your fastest path is to [narrow concrete move], then let real work tell you what to build next. You don't need the full stack on day one."

---

## 6. Quick acknowledgement / agreement / thanks
**Looks like:** Someone thanks him, agrees, or shares something small.
**Pattern:** Short, warm, often with an emoji. No name needed.
**Length:** One line.
**Examples:** "No worries.", "Perfect. That is good news.", "Thank you for sharing, I'll definitely try this one.", "yeah good idea, you can definitely do that 👌🙏"

---

## 7. Handoff / "I can't do this right now"
**Looks like:** A request for a 1:1, a call, or help he can't personally give immediately.
**Pattern:** Honest about availability + redirect to a Loom or a 1:1 call/space + keep it warm so they don't feel brushed off. Often apologizes for a late reply.
**Length:** 2-4 sentences.
**Example shape:** "Hey [Name], I'd love to do a one-on-one but I may not be able to right now. Could you record a quick Loom of what you've built and the problem you're hitting? Then I can go over it and send my thoughts. Sorry for the late reply."
