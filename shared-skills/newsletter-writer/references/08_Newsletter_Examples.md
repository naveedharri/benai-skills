# Newsletter Examples, Daily Emails & YouTube Promos (June–July 2026)

**This is the primary style reference. New newsletters should sound like THESE.**

All examples below were written for Ben AI's daily newsletter (sent via Kit) and reviewed/approved in the team's #funnel channel between June and July 2026. They represent the CURRENT voice and format of the newsletter.

Notes for reading these examples:
- `(firstname)` / `{{firstname}}` are Kit personalization merge tags.
- Bracketed letters like `[a]`, `[b]` at the end of some emails are internal Google Docs review comments between Ben and the team, NOT part of the email. They're kept because they show how the team thinks about copy decisions.
- "(Link)" and "Click here" are placeholders for the actual CTA links inserted in Kit.
- Some emails reference embedded screenshots/tweets that don't export to text.

## The anatomy shared by almost every daily email

1. **Subject line**: short, curiosity-driven, often a pattern interrupt or personalized tease (see 10_Subject_Lines.md)
2. **Cold open**: drop straight into a story, quote, question, or striking claim, no throat-clearing, no "welcome to this week's newsletter"
3. **The story**: a personal moment (travel, milestone, conversation), a famous story (Frank Abagnale, Gary Halbert, Anne Scheiber), a community member win, or something funny found online
4. **The turn**: "Anyways…" / "But here's the thing:" / "Now, let me be clear:", extract the lesson
5. **The bridge**: connect the lesson to AI / building systems / the reader's business
6. **The CTA**: soft, natural pitch for the Ben AI Accelerator ("Click here…"), often with a bulleted list of what's inside
7. **Sign-off**: "Keep going," + "Ben"
8. **Optional PS**: secondary pitch, callback joke, or extra resource

## You are setting the wrong goals, (firstname)

Just before I started my YouTube channel two years ago, I listened to a podcast with Chris Williamson and Alex Hormozi.


At the time, I was still debating whether I should start the channel at all.


However, during the podcast, Hormozi said something that stuck with me:


“Focus on inputs, not outputs.”


In other words, don’t focus on the results you can’t directly control, like subscribers, views, or sales.


Instead, focus on the things you can control, like how many videos you publish, how much outreach you do, and how often you actually sit down and do the work.


He finished his point off by saying:


“Do so much volume it would be unreasonable for you to fail.”


And that clicked for me.


Because back then, my mind was full of doubts about the whole YouTube thing.


I was worried that nobody would watch…


That the channel wouldn’t bring in any qualified leads…


And, more than anything, that my friends would stumble upon my content and think it was ridiculous…


Basically, I was obsessing over things I couldn’t control.

I figured that if I published 100 videos, it would be unreasonable for the channel to completely fail.


And it would probably also be unreasonable for me to still suck at making videos.


Fast forward to today, I am 97 videos in and it is impressive how everything seems to be coming together at the same time: 

I reached 200,000 subscribers on YouTube…


People are calling me a natural in front of the camera… (Which is nice, but also wrong)

Most people enter the space focused entirely on outcomes like saving 20 hours per week or making money selling AI solutions.


Sure, these goals are completely possible.


But the problem is that you have no idea how long they will take. 


Maybe AI only saves you two hours per week at first, instead of 20…

And over time, it will all come together.


But you need to set the right goals and get to work. 


Keep going,
