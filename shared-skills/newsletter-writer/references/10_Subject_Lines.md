# Subject Line Guide (current era, June/July 2026)

**The subject line philosophy changed.** The old approach was big-claim, benefit-driven subject lines ("How I built two $1M AI businesses as a Marketer", "The $10 Trillion AI Opportunity"). The current approach is the opposite: **short, human, curiosity-first**. The subject line's ONLY job is the open. The email earns the click.

## The rules

1. **Short wins.** Most winners are 2–6 words. If it doesn't fit on one line of a phone lock screen, cut it.
2. **Curiosity gap > benefit statement.** The subject should create a question the reader can only answer by opening ("The FBI was speechless", "Pointless", "This job post broke my brain"). Don't resolve the tension in the subject.
3. **Sound like a person, not a marketer.** Lowercase energy, conversational punctuation ("Wait… Is THIS a cop?", "About yesterday😬"). A subject that could plausibly come from a friend outperforms one that smells like a campaign.
4. **`(firstname)` inside a sentence, not as a greeting.** The strongest personalization puts the name mid-thought: "(firstname) is a natural", "You are setting the wrong goals, (firstname)", "Could I test this in your business, (firstname)?". Use in roughly 1 out of 4 emails, it loses power when overused.
5. **Direct beats soft.** When the team A/B-debated "You are setting the wrong goals, (firstname)" vs. the softer "Are you setting the wrong goals?", everyone picked the direct version. A little edge is on-brand; hedging is not.
6. **Humor and absurdity are allowed, and work.** "Kylie Jenner is my personal assistant", "AI secrets from a 101-Year-Old Woman". The email must pay the joke off with a real lesson.
7. **Never clickbait without payoff.** The open loop in the subject MUST be resolved early in the email, and the story must genuinely connect to the lesson. Curiosity, not deception.
8. **Emojis: rare, deliberate.** One emoji max, only when it adds tone ("About yesterday😬", "After 6 months? 🙁", "I saw what you did 🫣"). Most subjects have none.
9. **YouTube promo emails are the exception**, they can be slightly more descriptive/benefit-y because the product is the video: "How to ACTUALLY use skills", "Avoid this to get 10x better Claude responses". Caps for emphasis on ONE word is a recurring device (ACTUALLY, THIS).
10. **Preview text:** when provided, it complements rather than repeats the subject ("How to ACTUALLY use skills" → "Copy this simple 5-step framework to create powerful skills that actually work."). Suggest one for every subject line option.

## When generating options in the workflow

Generate 5–7 options per newsletter:
- 2 short curiosity/pattern-interrupt lines (2–5 words)
- 1–2 with `(firstname)` woven into the sentence
- 1 humorous/absurd angle (only if the email's content supports it)
- 1 direct statement or mild provocation ("Nobody wants your AI system")
- 1 wildcard (question, one-word, or emoji-toned)

For each option, add a one-line preview text suggestion. Flag your recommended pick and say why in one sentence.

## Anti-patterns (do not generate)

- Big-number claims: "How I built two $1M AI businesses" (old era, retired)
- Listicle-speak: "5 ways to…", "The ultimate guide to…"
- Hype vocabulary: "game-changing", "revolutionary", "insane", "🤯"
- Full-sentence benefit promises: "Build your $10K/month AI business in 90 days"
- ALL-CAPS whole subjects or multiple exclamation marks
- Fake urgency in dailies (urgency is reserved for real deadline campaigns)
