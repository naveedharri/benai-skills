## Voice rules for community announcement posts

These rules layer on top of `Context/brand.md` (Ben's public/marketing voice), `Context/ben-voice.md` (how Ben actually writes 1:1 to people, extracted from real DMs), and `Resources/frameworks/youtube-voice.md`. They are specific to Circle community announcement posts written from Ben's account.

> [!important] Read both voice sources first
> `Context/brand.md` has Voice DNA, Three Voice Modes, Language Rules, Signature Phrases, Sentence Rhythm, and Red Flags. `Context/ben-voice.md` has the actual cadence. how Ben writes when there's no marketing hat on. This file does NOT replace either. It adds the post-specific layer on top.

> [!info] Where community posts sit
> Community posts live between the two voices. Length-wise, closer to brand.md (longer than DMs). Tone-wise, closer to ben-voice.md (warmer, less polished, peer-to-peer). Use the sign-off "Keep going,\nBen" (per brand.md), even though Ben never signs off in DMs.

### Voice

- First person, Ben's voice. The post comes from his account.
- Practitioner authority: he built it, used it, has the numbers. Not "this resource will help you".
- Direct and conversational. Talk like a teammate, not an instructor.
- Anti-hype: no "game-changer", "blueprint", "ultimate", "unlock", "leverage", "harness", "supercharge".
- No filler openings ("Hey everyone", "Just dropped a new video", "Excited to share", "I'm thrilled to announce").
- Vulnerability before authority. If you have a relevant struggle or specific number, lead with it.
- **Loose, not polished** (from ben-voice.md). If the draft reads as overly clean, it's wrong. A stray capital or a slightly informal punctuation choice is fine. The goal is "a real person typing, not a brand team shipping copy".
- **Answer-first** (from ben-voice.md). Get to the point in the first line. Context comes after, not before.
- **Peer-to-peer warmth**: occasional use of "frankly", "to be honest", "genuinely", "very cool", "fantastic", or "glad to hear that". Sparingly. one of these per post, never three. Overuse strips the signal.

### Structure

1. **Hook** (1 to 2 sentences): the punchline of the video as a specific insight, not a summary. "The agent you're running six months from now is decided by the second brain you set up today." beats "In this video, I show you how to build a second brain."
2. **Body** (2 to 3 paragraphs): the framework or core idea, with concrete examples Ben referenced in the video. Real numbers, real names, real specifics.
3. **What you'll learn** (3 bullets, optional): specific outcomes, not vague promises. "How to set up a design system in 15 minutes" beats "Understanding design systems."
4. **Resources** (optional): tools, skills, courses, lead magnets the video referenced. Each as a short labelled block: name, one or two sentence description, "[grab it]" or "Check it out here." pointer that the user fills in when editing.
5. **Link**: full YouTube URL on its own line.
6. **Sign-off**: `Keep going,\nBen` on two lines.

For some videos (short utility videos, skill drops, template releases) the minimal form is appropriate: title, URL, resource list with one-line descriptions, no hook, no sign-off. Look at `2026-05-12-how-to-actually-use-claude-design-like-the-1.md` for the minimal pattern and `2026-05-16-os-stack-community-post.md` for the full pattern. Default to the full pattern unless the video is clearly a utility drop.

### Mechanics

- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, line breaks. This is a hard vault rule.
- One-sentence paragraphs for emphasis are good. Use them.
- Sentence rhythm: long setup with context, short punch, short punch, medium bridge to the next idea.
- Quote single specific numbers when they add credibility ("28-slide template", "under 15 minutes", "started with 30 docs, six weeks later hundreds").
- Wikilinks for any vault entity references.
- No exclamation points in body copy. They almost always read as hype.
- No emojis unless the user explicitly asks.

### Banned vocab (rewrite on sight)

From `brand.md` and `ben-voice.md` combined:

`powerful`, `comprehensive`, `incredible`, `amazing`, `ultimate`, `game-changing`, `unlock`, `leverage`, `harness`, `supercharge`, `revolutionize`, `transform your business`, `unleash`, `tremendous`, `next-level`, `cutting-edge`, `synergy`, `ecosystem`, `circling back`, `touching base`.

Phrases that mark the writing as AI/marketing slop, ban-on-sight:
- "In this video, I explore / we explore / we'll discover..."
- "This {resource|video|skill} will help you {unlock|leverage|supercharge}..."
- "I hope this finds you well..." / any corporate opener
- "Get ready to..."
- "Buckle up..."
- "Take your business to the next level."
- "I'm just a..." / "This means the world to me!" (fake humility, performative gratitude. from ben-voice.md)

### Good vs bad examples

**Bad** (sounds like content marketing, not Ben):
> Excited to share this comprehensive new video where I unpack the ultimate framework for leveraging AI in your business! In this video, you'll discover the game-changing strategies that will help you unlock the full potential of your second brain. Get ready to take your operations to the next level!

**Good** (sounds like Ben):
> The agent you're running six months from now is decided by the second brain you set up today.
>
> I started mine with 30 docs. Six weeks later, hundreds. Now it's thousands, auto-updated from Fireflies, Slack, and Circle. Every Cowork session, every Code routine, every team member runs against the same brain. The earlier you start, the more it compounds.
>
> Set it up this weekend. The compounding starts the day you do.

Notice in the good version: a specific concrete claim, a real number, a personal story, a short punchy close. No buzzwords. No hype. No exclamation points.

### Sniff test

Before saving, read the draft out loud. Ask:
- Does this sound like a practitioner talking to peers in his accelerator? (right)
- Does it sound like Ben texting back about something he built? (right)
- Or does it sound like a brand reply, a launch email, or an AI-generated announcement? (wrong)
- Does it sound "polished"? (wrong. see ben-voice.md: "If a draft sounds polished, it is wrong.")

If the answer skews wrong, rewrite. Look at the prior examples in `Community/community-posts/` and match their cadence and length.
